02 · Metastability

A flip-flop, caught mid-decision, can't be rushed.

Every flip-flop needs its input to be stable for a setup/hold window around the clock edge. Violate that window and the output doesn't cleanly resolve to 0 or 1 — it can hover, unresolved, for an unpredictable extra stretch of time. That's metastability, and it's why every clock-domain crossing gets its own synchroniser.

Setup/hold margin
Comfortable margin. Data settles well before the setup window — the flip-flop samples a clean, stable 1.
Why can't you just add more logic to "fix" a metastable output on the very next clock edge?
Right — resolution time is probabilistic, not instant. A two- or three-flop synchroniser doesn't eliminate metastability, it just makes the probability of it surviving long enough to cause a problem astronomically small.
