Several instructions at once
means they sometimes trip.

You'll build a processor in SystemVerilog and prove it on an FPGA. The single hardest idea to get real intuition for before you start: a pipeline runs several instructions at once, and sometimes they trip over each other.

01 · The Pipeline

Five instructions, five stages, one clock — but not one at a time.

Think of it like a kitchen line: one cook chops, the next fries, the next plates — all working simultaneously on different orders. Fast, until order 4 needs an ingredient order 2 hasn't finished chopping yet. That's a hazard. Step through this real 5-instruction sequence and watch one hazard get quietly fixed by forwarding, and another force a stall.

Why can forwarding fix the ADD→SUB hazard but not the LW→OR hazard?
Exactly — it's a timing race. An ALU result is ready one stage earlier than a memory-loaded value, and that single-stage gap is the entire reason load-use hazards need a stall while ALU-to-ALU hazards usually don't.
