01 · Physically Unclonable Functions

Two "identical" chips are never actually identical.

Manufacturing leaves microscopic, uncontrollable variation in every transistor — like fingerprints on identically-milled keys. A PUF turns that randomness into a function: feed it a "challenge," get a chip-unique "response." Same chip, same challenge, same response, always. Different chip, same challenge, different response — and nobody, not even the manufacturer, can predict it in advance or clone it into another chip.

Why is a PUF-derived key generally considered harder to extract than a key stored in ordinary on-chip flash memory?
Right. A stored key is a target sitting in memory. A PUF key is reconstructed from the chip's physical fingerprint each time it's needed and never persists — there's nothing to extract at rest, only a manufacturing accident an attacker would have to physically reverse-engineer.
