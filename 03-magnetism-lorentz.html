01 · Why Magnets Work at All

A magnet is billions of tiny compass needles, all pointing the same way.

Every electron spinning inside every atom acts like an impossibly tiny magnet, with its own north and south pole — a property called spin. In most materials, neighboring atoms' tiny magnetic fields point in random directions and cancel each other out completely. In materials like iron, cobalt, and nickel, though, groups of atoms called magnetic domains naturally line up with their neighbors. An unmagnetized piece of iron still has all these domains — they're just pointing every which way, like a crowd of compass needles scattered randomly, so their fields cancel out on average. Magnetize it, and every domain snaps into the same direction — suddenly billions of tiny fields point one way and add up into one strong field you can feel.

Domain Alignment
Every arrow above is a simplified magnetic domain. Aligned, they reinforce each other into one strong net field — exactly what happens when you stroke a steel needle repeatedly with a magnet to make your own compass.
02 · Magnetic Field Lines

Invisible loops that always run from North back to South.

A magnetic field is easiest to picture as a set of loops flowing out of the North pole, curving through the space around the magnet, and diving back in at the South pole — then continuing right through the inside of the magnet to close the loop. A compass needle anywhere nearby just rotates to align with whatever field line passes through it, exactly like a leaf caught in a stream orients itself with the current.

Compass Around a Bar Magnet
Every compass points along the local field direction. Notice they all curve from N around to S — never the other way, and never crossing each other.
03 · Electromagnetism

Moving charge doesn't just flow — it radiates a magnetic field for free.

Here's the deep connection: any moving electric charge creates a magnetic field around itself, automatically, as a direct consequence of relativity acting on electric fields (the full explanation is genuinely relativistic, but the everyday rule of thumb is simpler). Current flowing through a straight wire creates field loops circling around it — point your right thumb in the direction of current flow, and your curled fingers show the field's direction (the right-hand rule). Coil that wire into a loop, and all those circular fields stack up inside the loop into one strong, directional field — you've just built an electromagnet.

Right-Hand Rule: Straight Wire
Point your right thumb up (the current's direction) and your fingers curl counter-clockwise viewed from above — that's exactly the direction these field rings circle.

04 · Electromagnetic Induction (a preview)

Run the whole idea backwards: a changing field creates current.

If current creates a magnetic field, the reverse is also true — a magnetic field that's changing near a wire induces a voltage in that wire (Faraday's Law). Critically, it's the change that matters, not the field itself: a magnet sitting still next to a coil, no matter how strong, induces nothing at all. Only a magnet moving relative to the coil — its field strength at the coil changing moment to moment — pushes electrons into motion. This is the entire principle behind generators, transformers, and regenerative braking. Chapter 1.4 goes deep on this — Faraday's Law properly stated, Lenz's Law, eddy currents, and hysteresis. This is just enough to finish the story of "moving charge ↔ magnetic field" before the Lorentz force ties both directions together below.

05 · The Lorentz Force

The one equation that explains every motor, speaker, and CRT ever built.

A charged particle moving through a magnetic field feels a force — but a strange one: it doesn't push along the direction of motion or along the field, it pushes perpendicular to both at once. This is the Lorentz force:

F = qv × B

where q is the charge, v is its velocity, and B is the magnetic field. Because the force is always perpendicular to the velocity, it never speeds the particle up or slows it down — it only steers it, bending its path into a circle, like a ball on a string being swung around your head by a constant sideways pull.

Charged Particle in a Field
Release the particle and watch it curve into a circle, not a straight line. Flip the charge sign or the field direction and the circle bends the opposite way — that's the right-hand rule (or left-hand for negative charge) doing exactly what it does inside a CRT tube or a mass spectrometer.
This same equation, unmodified, is what steers electron beams in an old CRT television (6.3), what focuses a mass spectrometer, and what confines plasma in a fusion reactor. One cross product, four very different machines.
06 · The Motor Principle

Zoom out from one charge to a whole wire, and you get a motor.

A current-carrying wire is just billions of moving charges, so the Lorentz force applies to the whole wire at once — the field pushes the entire wire sideways with a force F = BIL (field × current × wire length). This is the exact mechanism spinning every electric motor that exists — from a desk fan (6.7) to an EV drivetrain: current in a wire sitting inside a magnetic field feels a sideways shove, and if that wire is wound around a rotor, the shove becomes rotation.

Force on a Current-Carrying Wire
Flip the current direction and watch the force arrow flip too — this single reversal, timed by the inverter in 2.3, is what keeps a motor's rotor continuously pushed in the direction it's already spinning.
Quick Check
A charged particle moves in a straight line with no magnetic field present. A field is then switched on, aligned exactly parallel to its velocity. What happens?
Right — the Lorentz force depends on v × B, and the cross product of two parallel vectors is zero. A charge moving exactly along the field feels no force at all; only the velocity component perpendicular to the field gets deflected.

Electricity and magnetism were never two separate things.

Moving charge makes a field. A changing field pushes charge. Every motor, generator, speaker, and transformer later in this bible is just this one relationship, wired up in a particular shape.
