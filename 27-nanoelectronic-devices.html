Zoom all the way in.
It's always a MOSFET.

Every "1" and "0" everywhere else in this bible is, physically, a switch built from exactly this: a MOSFET deciding whether current flows based on how much voltage is stacked on its gate.

01 · The MOSFET as a Valve

Gate voltage opens the tap. Drain voltage decides how fast it flows.

Below threshold, the tap is shut — negligible current (cutoff). Above threshold, current rises with drain voltage until the channel "pinches off," after which more drain voltage barely matters — the tap is fully open and current plateaus (saturation). In between is triode: a nearly linear resistor you control with the gate.

Try this: set V_GS below 1.0V — the device is in cutoff no matter what V_DS does. That's threshold voltage: the gate hasn't inverted the channel yet, so there's simply no conducting path.
Why this module exists beyond one equation: as transistors shrink, this clean square-law model starts lying — threshold voltage drops unpredictably (short-channel effects), leakage current in "off" state stops being negligible, and gate oxide gets so thin that high-k dielectrics and FinFET 3D gates become necessary just to keep the switch behaving like a switch. That's the scaling story this module's TCAD labs are built around.
At fixed V_GS = 2.0V, why does I_D stop rising once V_DS passes (V_GS − V_th)?
Correct — pinch-off caps the current the channel can deliver at that gate overdrive. Past it you're in saturation: I_D depends on V_GS, but is only weakly sensitive to further increases in V_DS (real devices show a slight slope from channel-length modulation, which TCAD lets you see directly).

02 · The Exam Toolkit

The simulator shows the behaviour. The exam wants the equations behind it.

1 · Threshold voltage — what it's built from
V_th = V_FB + 2φ_F + √(2·ε_si·q·N_A·2φ_F) / C_ox
TermMeaning
V_FBFlat-band voltage — gate/body work-function difference
2φ_FSurface potential needed for strong inversion
N_ABody doping concentration
C_oxOxide capacitance per area — thinner oxide, higher C_ox

Exam pattern: give you N_A, oxide thickness, and work functions, ask you to compute V_th numerically. Practice this arithmetic — it's mechanical once you know which term is which.

2 · Subthreshold slope
S = (kT/q)·ln(10)·(1 + C_dm/C_ox) [mV/decade]

The physical floor at room temperature (300K), with C_dm/C_ox → 0, is:

S_ideal ≈ 60 mV/decade

No MOSFET beats this without new physics (tunnel-FETs, negative capacitance) — a real device is always somewhat worse than 60mV/decade because C_dm/C_ox > 0. One of the most quoted facts in the whole module — know it cold.

3 · Constant-field (Dennard) scaling — the table examiners love

Shrink every dimension by a factor 1/κ (κ > 1) and scale voltage the same way, and see what happens to everything else:

QuantityScaling
Dimensions (L, W, t_ox)1/κ
Voltage V1/κ
Doping concentrationκ
Current I1/κ
Capacitance C1/κ
Delay (∝ CV/I)1/κ
Power per gate (∝ CV²f)1/κ²
Power densityconstant
Why this became famous: "power density constant" was the whole promise of Dennard scaling — shrink the transistor, get it faster and lower power, for free. It broke down in the mid-2000s once leakage and voltage couldn't keep scaling down — that breakdown is exactly why high-k dielectrics and FinFETs became necessary rather than optional.
4 · High-k dielectrics — the leakage fix

As t_ox shrinks toward a handful of atomic layers, electrons start quantum-mechanically tunnelling straight through the gate oxide — gate leakage current, scaling exponentially with thickness. Swap SiO₂ (k≈3.9) for a high-k material (e.g. HfO₂, k≈20+) and you keep the same Equivalent Oxide Thickness — EOT = t_high-k · (3.9/k_high-k) — same C_ox, same V_th behaviour — while the physical layer stays thick enough to suppress tunnelling.

5 · FinFETs — the electrostatics fix

A planar MOSFET's gate only controls the channel from one side — as channel length shrinks, the drain's field starts reaching the source and controlling the channel too (eroding V_th, increasing leakage). A FinFET wraps the gate around a thin vertical silicon "fin" on three sides, giving the gate far stronger control relative to the drain — this is what lets channel length keep shrinking without V_th collapsing.

How these five pieces connect for the exam: V_th and subthreshold slope are the numbers you calculate; Dennard scaling is the historical narrative that explains why those numbers had to keep improving; high-k and FinFETs are the two specific engineering fixes that kept the narrative going once naive scaling broke. A strong answer on any one of these should reference where it sits in that chain.
